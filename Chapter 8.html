8.1 Digital Society and Computer Ethics

A digital society is a society where computers, the internet, and digital technologies are widely used for communication, education, business, entertainment, and other activities. Computer ethics refers to the moral principles and rules that guide the responsible use of computers and technology.

Ethical Issues in Computing include privacy violations, unauthorized access, software piracy, plagiarism, misuse of personal information, and spreading false or harmful information.

Computer Crimes are illegal activities carried out using computers or digital networks. Examples include hacking, identity theft, online fraud, unauthorized access, and data theft.

Security Measures such as strong passwords, antivirus software, firewalls, encryption, regular updates, and data backups help protect computer systems and information from misuse and attacks.

8.2 Concept of Information Security

Information security is the practice of protecting information and information systems from unauthorized access, use, modification, disclosure, damage, or destruction. It aims to keep information safe, accurate, and available to authorized users.

Threats to Information Security include malware, hacking, phishing, weak passwords, unauthorized access, data theft, and hardware or software failures.

Encryption is the process of converting readable information into an unreadable form called ciphertext so that unauthorized people cannot understand it.

Decryption is the process of converting encrypted information back into its original readable form using an appropriate key or method.

8.3 Concept of Cybercrime

Cybercrime refers to illegal activities performed using computers, digital devices, or computer networks. Cybercriminals may target individuals, organizations, computer systems, or digital information.

Types of Cybercrime include hacking, identity theft, phishing, online fraud, cyberbullying, data theft, malware attacks, and unauthorized access to computer systems.

Cyber Laws are laws created to regulate activities involving computers, digital systems, electronic communication, and the internet. They help prevent cybercrime and provide legal consequences for unlawful activities.

Implications of Cybercrime can include financial loss, loss of personal information, damage to reputation, privacy violations, disruption of services, and legal consequences.

8.4 Malicious Software and Spam

Malicious software, or malware, is software designed to damage computer systems, steal information, disrupt operations, or gain unauthorized access.

  • Virus - A program that can attach itself to other files and spread when the infected file is executed.
  • Worm - Malware that can copy itself and spread across computers or networks without requiring a user to manually copy it.
  • Trojan Horse - Malicious software that appears to be legitimate or useful but performs harmful actions when executed.
  • Spyware - Software designed to secretly monitor activities or collect information from a device.
  • Ransomware - Malware that prevents access to files or systems and demands payment from the victim.

Phishing is a technique in which attackers use fake messages, websites, or emails to trick people into revealing sensitive information such as passwords or account details.

Spam refers to unwanted and usually unsolicited messages sent in large numbers, often through email or other communication platforms. Spam may contain advertisements, scams, or harmful links.

Preventive Measures include avoiding suspicious links and attachments, using updated security software, checking website addresses carefully, using strong passwords, and keeping operating systems and applications updated.

8.5 Protection from Cybercrime

Cybersecurity involves protecting computers, networks, applications, and information from digital threats. Good cybersecurity practices reduce the risk of unauthorized access, malware infections, data loss, and other cyberattacks.

  • Strong Passwords - Use long and unique passwords for different accounts.
  • Firewall - A security system that monitors and controls network traffic entering or leaving a computer or network.
  • Antivirus Software - Software that detects, blocks, and removes many types of malicious software.
  • Software Updates - Keeping software updated helps fix security weaknesses and improve protection.
  • Data Backup - Making copies of important data allows information to be restored if the original data is lost, damaged, or attacked.

8.6 Intellectual Property Right

Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) are legal rights that protect creations and original works of individuals or organizations. They help creators control how their work is used and prevent unauthorized copying or misuse.

  • Copyright - Protects original creative works such as books, music, software, photographs, videos, and other digital content.
  • Patent - Provides legal protection for a new invention or technological innovation and gives the inventor certain exclusive rights.
  • Trademark - Protects names, logos, symbols, or signs that identify and distinguish the goods or services of a particular organization.

Importance of IPR - Intellectual property rights encourage creativity and innovation by giving creators legal protection over their work. They also help prevent unauthorized copying, distribution, and commercial use.

8.7 Concept of Digital Signature

A digital signature is an electronic method used to verify the authenticity and integrity of a digital message or document. It helps confirm that the document came from the claimed sender and has not been altered after signing.

Digital signatures use cryptographic techniques and are commonly used in electronic documents, online transactions, and digital communication.

8.8 Concept of Cyber Law in Nepal

Cyber law in Nepal refers to the laws and legal provisions that regulate the use of computers, electronic communication, digital transactions, and the internet in Nepal.

The Electronic Transactions Act, 2063 (2006) is an important law related to electronic transactions and cyber activities in Nepal. It provides legal recognition to electronic records and digital signatures and addresses various computer-related offences.

Cyber laws help protect users, regulate digital activities, and provide legal action against activities such as unauthorized access, electronic fraud, and misuse of digital systems.

8.9 ICT Policy in Nepal

ICT policy refers to government policies and plans designed to promote the development and use of Information and Communication Technology in the country.

In Nepal, ICT policies aim to expand digital infrastructure, improve digital services, promote ICT education and skills, encourage innovation, and increase the use of technology in areas such as education, health, government, and business.

ICT policies also focus on improving cybersecurity, promoting digital inclusion, and making government services more accessible through digital technologies.
